Thermodynamics of spacetime & entropic gravity

In 1995, Ted Jacobson showed the Einstein field equations are not fundamental — they are a macroscopic thermodynamic equation of state. Assuming the Clausius relation δQ = T·dS holds across all local Rindler horizons:

\[\delta Q = \int T_{ab}\,k^a k^b\,dV\,d\lambda \qquad T = \frac{\hbar a}{2\pi c k_B}\qquad S = \frac{c^3}{4G\hbar}A\]

Heat flux across the horizon, Unruh temperature, Bekenstein–Hawking entropy. Combine with the Raychaudhuri equation and curvature is forced to satisfy:

\[R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}\]

Erik Verlinde's entropic gravity goes further: gravity as an entropic force, F·Δx = T·ΔS, from entropy gradients on holographic screens.

Where it strains: entropic gravity frames the cosmos as an inert stochastic machine driven by thermodynamic decay. It needs ad-hoc screening functions for dark-matter phenomena and cannot touch the 120-order-of-magnitude cosmological constant catastrophe.
